## Technical proof confirmed

`__constructor` had already configured the contract and set the contract itself as administrator.
Even so, `initialize` accepted later calls and replaced that configuration. The lesson is
**reinitialization**: the contract does not prevent an initialization routine from changing critical
state after the initial setup.

Complete configuration atomically in `__constructor`. If a separate routine is necessary, protect it
with a one-time initialization guard and an authorized setup flow; requiring the proposed address to
sign does not prevent later calls by itself.

After taking over the administrator role, reading the record enabled `release`. Bytes stored on the
ledger are public: authorization controls the call, not data confidentiality. The record was the
clue, not the prize. It points to Warehouse 17.

The record carried a destination:

> `WAREHOUSE 17 // LOWER MERIDIAN // DO NOT USE CITY ROUTING`

Mara recognizes the name. Warehouse 17 should not exist.
